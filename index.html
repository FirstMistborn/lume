How-to

Make it feel expensive

  1. 1

    Consent & check-ins

    Ask before you start, and again after a minute: “more pressure, less, or stay?” Talking is part of the technique.

  2. 2

    Setup

    Warm room, towel under them, another towel to cover when you finish. Phone on silent. Oil or unscented lotion in a dish — not poured cold onto skin.

  3. 3

    Warm your hands

    Rub oil between palms until they’re warm. Cold hands kill the mood instantly.

  4. 4

    Direction

    Long strokes on the back generally travel up toward the heart (low back → shoulders). Work the muscle beside the spine, never grind on the vertebrae.

  5. 5

    Use your body, not just arms

    Lean from the hips. Soft elbows. If your forearms burn after two minutes, you’re muscling it — slow down and use weight.

  6. 6

    Don’t break contact

    When you switch sides or strokes, keep one hand on them. The nervous system reads “still safe.”

  7. 7

    Finish like a pro

    Last 60 seconds go lighter and slower. Cover with a towel. Give them 30 seconds before they have to sit up.

Skip these

Fresh injury, unexplained pain, fever, blood clots, pregnancy (unless you know prenatal work), directly on the spine or kidneys. When in doubt, stay light and stay off.

Library

The strokes

Effleurage — long glides

Open palms, whole hand. Spreads oil, warms tissue, maps the back. This is most of the “arrow video.” Slow. Repeat 4–6 times each path.

Paravertebral — beside the spine

Thumbs or palms either side of the bony ridge, traveling up. The money line for “don’t stop.” Keep 1–2 cm off the vertebrae.

Fan-out — ribs & lats

From the mid-back, glide out toward the sides following the rib angle. Opens the breath. Lighter over the floating ribs.

Traps & shoulders

Knead the upper traps (the coat-hanger muscles). Thumb circles on the meat of the shoulder, not the pointy acromion.

Petrissage — knead

Pick up and roll muscle like bread dough. Best on traps and outer back, not on thin tissue over bone.

Friction — small circles

Thumb or two fingers, tiny circles on a tight spot. 20–30 seconds, then glide away. Ask if the “hurt-so-good” is still good.
